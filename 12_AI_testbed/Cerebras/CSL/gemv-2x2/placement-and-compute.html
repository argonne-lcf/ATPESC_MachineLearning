Where A, x, b live — and how each PE computes

The problem is y = A·x + b with A 4×6, split across four PEs. Pick a PE below to see exactly which slice of the data it holds, then watch it turn that slice into its piece of the answer.

1 Placement — who holds what

A is carved into four quadrants (one per PE). x is loaded only on the top row; b only on the left column. Hover or tap a PE to light up its slice in the matrix.

Columns are grouped 0–2 · 3–5  |  rows 0–1 · 2–3

Note: the top row holds x; the bottom row gets its copy by broadcast (south). b sits in the left column so it rides the eastward reduce into the final answer.

2 Computation — the column view

A matrix–vector product is a weighted sum of A's columns: y = Σ x[j]·A[:,j]. Each PE only sums its own columns. Every x value arrives as a wavelet and triggers one multiply-accumulate (@fmacs).

3 Reduce east — into the final answer

Each row of PEs adds its left partial into its right partial (the ax_color reduce). The right column then holds the finished y, which the host reads back.

A = arange(24).reshape(4,6) · x = ones(6) · b = twos(4)  —  expected y = A@x + b = [17, 53, 89, 125]
